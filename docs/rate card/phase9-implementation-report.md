# Phase 9 Implementation Report — Rider Final Settlement

## Existing components reused

Phase 3 agreement and immutable commercial versions; operational allocation deallocation, post-deallocation inspections and evidence; Phase 4 deposit transactions, balances, refund requests and liability ledger; Phase 6 billing schedule, frozen-version proration, invoices, credits, payment allocation and reconciliation; Phase 7 provider payment refunds; Phase 8 invoice-led collections; existing audit log, client context and role guards. No additional billing, payment, collections or ledger engine was introduced.

## Prisma models, indexes and constraints

Added `RentalTerminationRequest`, `RiderFinalSettlement`, `SettlementRevision`, `SettlementChargeAssessment`, `SettlementDepositHold`, `SettlementDepositApplication`, `SettlementRefundRequest`, `SettlementAdjustment`, `RiderFinancialNote`, `SettlementSequence`, `RiderSettlementCreditApplication`, and `ClientSettlementPolicy`. Agreement now has `commercialClosureState` and `financiallyClosedAt`; invoice has `appliedSettlementCreditAmount`; rider payment has `refundedAmount`. Settlement and note numbers use per-client financial-year sequences and composite uniqueness. Settlement child foreign keys include `clientId`; the database enforces one settlement and one termination request per agreement. The migration extends the existing invoice balance constraint to account for applied settlement credits while leaving invoice `totalAmount` intact; it also bounds refunded payment amounts.

## Termination, return and final billing

A termination request records the intended date, reason and idempotency key separately from actual return. The return endpoint requires an existing completed allocation deallocation and completed `POST_DEALLOCATION` inspection for the agreement's current vehicle. It records the deallocation timestamp as the rental liability cutoff and changes the rental status to `TERMINATED`, while financial closure remains separate. The Phase 6 billing engine caps the final period at that cutoff, uses the commercial version effective in the period, applies its existing `proratedAmount` function against the full natural period, and closes the schedule. Approved post-return assessments and settlement notes enter that final invoice through Phase 6 source records even when approval occurs after the service cutoff. A schedule is required for settlement approval, and an already billed period cannot be billed again.

## Charge assessment, notes and adjustment

Assessments retain a type, source, evidence references, assessed amount, approved amount and separate approver. Only approved assessments post a Phase 6 rider charge; rejected findings have no financial effect. Credit and debit notes have per-client sequences and immutable issued rows. Their effects post through existing Phase 6 credit/charge records with unique source keys. Credit applications to issued invoices are separate records and reduce `outstandingAmount` plus `appliedSettlementCreditAmount`; original invoice total and lines are unchanged. For taxable invoice notes, the implementation accepts only an invoice with one frozen taxable charge line and no invoice credit line, deriving the note tax split from that line's frozen tax. More complex tax corrections are rejected for finance review. Internal settlement adjustments require a separate approver and post through the same Phase 6 sources.

## Calculation, deposit, refunds and payment

Preview reads outstanding agreement invoices, final rental estimate, open settlement charges, available rider credits, confirmed unallocated payments, actual deposit balances and active holds without writing financial records. Settlement approval completes Phase 6 billing, applies available credits to issued invoices, allocates confirmed payments to that agreement's invoices, freezes a calculation snapshot and creates a revision record. The Client settlement policy must explicitly enable deposit application. Admin-authorized application uses the Phase 4 locked deduction path and a linked internal Phase 6 payment allocation, so both deposit liability and invoice receivable decrease in their existing ledgers. Holds reserve deposit balance without changing the original deposit; release enables a later partial refund. Phase 4 refund requests reserve refundable balance; the deposit ledger changes only when a client admin confirms a payout reference. Failed requests release their reservation for a new keyed attempt. Phase 7's existing provider refund service handles fully unallocated provider payments; partial or non-provider excess payments use a manually confirmed payout that updates the Phase 6 payment balance and posts a reversal-side ledger entry. Reserved manual refunds cannot be allocated to invoices while pending.

## Reconciliation, closure, collections and statement

Reconciliation invokes Phase 6 and Phase 4 checks, checks every agreement invoice including settlement credit applications, and verifies each deposit application against its deduction transaction and invoice allocations. Closure requires no agreement invoice outstanding, no deposit balance or active hold, no unresolved refund, no open settlement charge, and no unallocated payment or credit. Only then does it mark the settlement `SETTLED` and the agreement `FINANCIALLY_CLOSED`. Unpaid final invoices remain ordinary Phase 6 invoices and are picked up by Phase 8 collections; no second debt or dunning case is created. Operations and rider statements assemble existing agreements, vehicle version history, invoices, notes, payments, deposits, settlement and ledger records. Rider APIs bind to the authenticated rider and omit internal notes; evidence exposure is limited to reference IDs.

## APIs, RBAC, audit and concurrency

Operations APIs under `/api/v1/client/agreements/:agreementId` and `/api/v1/client/settlements/:id` cover preview, termination, return, settlement creation/read/review/approval, assessments, notes, adjustments, holds, deposit application, refund requests/completion/failure, reconciliation and closure. `/api/v1/client/settlement-policy` configures deposit application and manual refund capability. `/api/v1/client/riders/:riderId/financial-statement` serves operations. `/api/v1/rider-app/final-settlements/:id` and `/api/v1/rider-app/financial-statement` serve rider-safe views. Existing `CLIENT_ADMIN`, `OPERATIONS_MANAGER` and `RIDER` guards apply; admin-only financial decisions use maker-checker for assessed charges, adjustments and settlement approval. Audit rows record key lifecycle and financial transitions. Unique keys, serializable transactions, Phase 4 deposit row locks and existing Phase 6/7 source keys protect repeat posting. The PostgreSQL scenario checked concurrent settlement creation and repeat refund requests.

## Known limitations

Deposit payouts use an externally confirmed transfer reference; no bank or UPI payout provider is configured for deposits. Provider refunds are available only for a fully unallocated payment through Phase 7. Approval threshold configuration and higher-tier approver routing are not implemented; maker-checker is enforced for core decisions. Taxed notes with multiple taxable lines or mixed credit lines are rejected pending an explicit line-level tax correction workflow. An approved settlement is immutable; a correction after approval needs a new controlled financial workflow rather than editing an issued invoice. The revision table preserves the approved calculation but does not yet offer post-approval reopening. Settlement-specific metrics are not emitted; audit records provide observability. Deposit application composes existing Phase 4 and Phase 6 transactions using stable keys, so an infrastructure failure between those transactions may require an idempotent retry before reconciliation and closure. The application database was not migrated because its earlier nearest-hub migration requires PostGIS.
